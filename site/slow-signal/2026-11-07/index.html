Metabolic Mind @Metabolic_Mind · Nov 6Ideas & Life

🧠 People with serious mental illness can take on and stick with ketogenic therapy, according to feasibility results from a new randomized controlled trial from researchers out of James Cook University and Townsville Hospital and Health Service

The next paper will report psychiatric, cognitive, and metabolic outcomes.

Baszucki Group @BaszuckiGroup

The first results from the North Queensland Dietary Intervention Trial (NQDIT) are out in Frontiers in Nutrition.

This randomized controlled trial of ketogenic metabolic therapy (KMT) in schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder and bipolar disorder was led by researchers at James Cook University and Townsville Hospital and Health Service, including Dr. Calogero Longhitano (@doncaloger0), Dr. Jaymee-Leigh Swift, Prof. Zoltan Sarnyai (@ZSarnyai) and colleagues.

The feasibility study reports that KMT is a feasible and safe therapeutic dietary intervention for individuals with serious mental illness and that high adherence was achieved under real-world conditions with self-prepared meals.

The psychiatric, cognitive, and metabolic outcomes will follow in a forthcoming paper.

Congratulations to the NQDIT team. Baszucki Group is honored to have supported this work.

Preethi Kasireddy @iam_preethi · Nov 6Science & Health

Nobody talks about the fact that PCOS has subtypes.

A study of 11,908 women found 4 subtypes, each with different problems.

High androgen: highest risk of second trimester loss
Obesity: worst metabolic outcomes, with 16% developing type 2 diabetes over 6.5 years
High SHBG: mildest, with the best pregnancy outcomes
High LH and AMH: highest risk of ovarian overstimulation during IVF

The researchers used 9 measurements to decide which subtype each woman fell into. 8 were labs, including testosterone, SHBG, LH, AMH, and fasting insulin. The 9th was BMI.

Yet most women still get the same answer for all 4 types, which is birth control and "lose weight"
