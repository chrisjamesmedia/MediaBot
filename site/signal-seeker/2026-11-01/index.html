The AI money gets harder to raise: OpenAI cuts its round to about $20 billion

OpenAI has lowered the target for its latest funding round from $30 billion to about $20 billion, at a hoped-for valuation of $1.25 trillion, the Financial Times reported. The Wall Street Journal said about $11 billion is secured so far and described investor response as tepid; a UAE group including MGX is the largest participant, and a retail offering is planned. In the same week, Nvidia was reported to be weighing a guarantee of debt for Nscale's West Virginia data center, whose first 460 MW building is tied to a $44.6 billion Anthropic contract.

CNBC (FT report) · TechCrunch · TechCrunch (Nscale)

Intel's weak outlook sets off a global chip rout

Intel shares fell about 29% on Friday, on course for the company's worst day ever, after it guided fourth-quarter revenue to $15–16 billion and adjusted earnings to $0.25 a share, well below the $17.1 billion and $0.43 analysts expected. CEO Lip-Bu Tan blamed a severe PC slowdown driven by soaring memory costs and shortages, plus an Israel fab shutdown that could cost about $600 million in revenue over two quarters. Together with worries over Amazon's spending, the warning dragged chip and memory stocks sharply lower.

CNBC · CNBC (analysts) · Reuters via Investing.com

Iran threatens a response with "no geographic limits" as tankers burn in Hormuz

Iran's supreme leader vowed that any further attack would draw a response with no geographic limits, and Iran's Revolutionary Guard reported three tankers on fire in the Strait of Hormuz. AP also published an analysis of Iran's strikes on 26 U.S. bases in the region.

AP · AP summary via Times Argus

Big Tech is now competing with the Treasury for capital

On the All-In podcast, Chamath Palihapitiya and David Friedberg estimated that the financing the largest AI spenders need through 2027 could equal roughly 30% of U.S. Treasury issuance. Bill Ackman made a related case separately: he compared pure-play neoclouds to highly levered real-estate developers and expects that heavy competition for debt keeps rates high.

The bull reply: judge the spend by its return, not by free cash flow

VJN Capital argues Amazon's headline free cash flow hides about $145 billion of trailing growth capex; on its "owner FCF" measure, cash generation grew 36.8%, while the cloud backlog rose 16.5% quarter on quarter and 189% year on year. Bill Ackman says Amazon, Meta and Microsoft now make up about a third of his portfolio.

Neoclouds

Neoclouds: locked-in backlog versus spot pricing

Daniel argues CoreWeave's bigger issue is the upside it gave up by contracting capacity early: deals signed in early 2026 brought roughly $10–15 million per megawatt, while some premium contracts now reach $50 million. Nebius keeps part of its capacity for higher-priced short-term deals. Financing is the other test: Lambda, with a $50 billion backlog ($35 billion tied to Anthropic), has reportedly cut its pre-money target from $14.5 billion to $13 billion.

Memory & chips

Memory is now the most profitable business in chips

SK Hynix posted an 80% operating margin last quarter on 104.6 trillion won of revenue, above TSMC's 59%; in early 2023 its margin was minus 67%. From Nov. 1 it has a new Americas headquarters led by its HBM chief, Ahn Hyun, alongside a $4 billion packaging plant in Indiana. In Korea, chip startup FuriosaAI raised $620 million at a $2.9 billion valuation, with its next chip planned on 2 nm with HBM4.

An AI-agent hacking campaign hit 200 organizations in 11 days, CrowdStrike says

CrowdStrike says a China-linked group used AI agents, running mainly on DeepSeek, to target more than 200 organizations in 14 countries in 11 days, including 19 in the U.S. About 70% were compromised. Science & Health

NASA's Swift telescope is days from falling back to Earth

NASA ended the Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory's mission on Oct. 20 after its orbit decayed too quickly; a Katalyst Space rescue craft launched in July failed to boost it. Reentry is expected around Nov. 4, with a wide uncertainty window. Most of the 1.5-ton telescope should burn up, though some tungsten and mirror parts may survive.

NASA · AP · Space.com

A new lever on LDL cholesterol, so far only in mice

A Nature study found that high-cholesterol diets cut liver LDL receptors through a Ral/CTSA pathway; blocking CTSA lowered LDL in mice. Whether it works in people is untested.

ScienceDaily

A 2.8-million-person study maps 48 new Alzheimer's risk regions

A genetic study of 2.8 million people, published in Nature, identified 127 regions linked to Alzheimer's risk, 48 of them new, pointing to the brain's immune cells (microglia) as well as neurons as key players.

Medical Daily

How a common gut bacterium can set up colon cancer

Johns Hopkins researchers report in Nature that the toxin from Bacteroides fragilis damages the colon by acting on the protein claudin-4; a decoy molecule protected mice from the toxin's effects.
