Assorted things I've been mulling over regarding agents in the economy

Patrick Collison @patrickc

Stripe's co-founder argues that companies are the original superintelligence, profiting from consumers' bounded rationality, and that personal AI agents could weaken price discrimination and cross-subsidies while acting as a structural subsidy for product quality. He leaves open whether agents produce superstar effects or more dispersion, and who controls how demand is routed. His tentative conclusion: the rewards for simply making better products may go up.

X Article · Sep 28

OpenAI Understands Something Important and Rare

David George (a16z) @DavidGeorge83

Starting from Drucker's line that the purpose of a business is to create a customer, George walks through four levers: new behavior, distribution, pricing and switching costs. At the model layer switching costs are a Red Queen's race and pricing is competitive, so the durable edge is creating new behavior and owning distribution, where he argues OpenAI leads, with ChatGPT itself a happy accident.

Building the infrastructure is not the same as capturing the profits

Rose Celine Investments @realroseceline

A three-part thread on why the builders of a technological revolution don't necessarily keep its profits. It separates payback from lifetime return on capital and asks you to count replacement capex, financing costs and the spread between return and cost of capital, using Nebius as the worked example.
