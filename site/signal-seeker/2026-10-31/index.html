The AI spending bill comes due: Alphabet's worst day ever drags Wall Street to its worst week since June

Alphabet shares fell roughly 13% on Thursday, erasing about $500 billion in value — the stock's worst day on record — after search revenue (+12% to $63.35B) missed estimates, free cash flow swung to −$9.07B, and Google lifted its capex outlook to $195–205B. Amazon followed with a slight revenue and EPS miss and a deeply negative trailing free cash flow despite AWS growing 39% — its fastest in about five years. On Friday the S&P 500 fell 1.8% to 7,580.07 and the Nasdaq 2.9% to 25,437.00, capping a third straight losing week.

Anthropic files to go public, revealing $16.3B in first-half revenue — and an unprecedented governance structure

Anthropic's 300-plus-page S-1, filed Monday for a Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT, shows $16.29B in revenue for the first half of 2026 (vs. $1.16B a year earlier) at an implied ~85% gross margin. Growth slowed from ~144% quarter-on-quarter in Q2 to ~47% in Q3. Seven co-founders hold a single Class F share carrying 50.1% of shareholder voting power, while the Long-Term Benefit Trust elects a board majority; the company says the structure has 'no direct public company precedent.' Analysts cited by CNBC expect a valuation of $1–2 trillion.

AI can now write working exploits for every major browser and OS — and the 'Hackening' has started

Anthropic disclosed that an unreleased model chained bugs into working exploits against Chrome (62 CVEs fixed in Chrome 156, including 13 sandbox escapes), Safari, Android, Windows and Linux — including a remote-root Linux bug via MPTCP found in under an hour. All were patched before disclosure. In the same week, an AI-assisted 'PHP2Shell' campaign rooted servers including US government systems, white-hat group Cathedral reached some of Anthropic's own business systems through third-party tools, and a tampered Claude Code extension in the Cursor marketplace tried to plant persistent SSH access before being pulled Oct. 29.

OpenAI says its model refuted the Exponential Time Hypothesis; experts are still checking

OpenAI announced Oct. 26 that an internal model produced a Lean-verified refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis — a 27-year-old cornerstone of complexity theory — plus a proof of local smoothing in all dimensions that implies the Kakeya and Stein restriction conjectures. Anthropic reported a refutation of its own. Independent review by Impagliazzo, Paturi and Williams is under way; Lean confirms the proof is internally valid, not that its formal statement matches textbook ETH.

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic back one robots.txt rule to opt out of AI training

The four companies endorsed a shared IETF-track robots.txt 'usage' token that lets sites opt out of AI training with a single rule, rather than blocking a zoo of crawler names one by one. Meta said it was supportive. Site owners may still face trade-offs between training opt-outs and appearing in AI search answers.

Science & Health

A personalized mRNA cancer vaccine succeeds in a phase III trial

Moderna and Merck's personalized melanoma vaccine intismeran autogene, given with Keytruda after surgery, cut the risk of recurrence or death by 42% vs. Keytruda alone (HR 0.58) in 1,137 patients with high-risk melanoma. Two-year recurrence-free survival was 82.2% vs. 70.0%. Overall survival data are not yet mature.
