Alexandr Wang: "Superintelligence is arriving," and the path is not inevitable

Meta's chief AI officer published an X Article arguing that the Riemann Hypothesis proof marks the start of a real change: the gaps left for models are no longer intelligence but know-how, plumbing and experimentation, and AI research itself is easier than that proof, so models will likely be able to automate it. It drew the biggest response in his feed overnight. After critics quoted his line that it is hard to imagine humanity keeping control over unconstrained recursive self-improvement, he followed up that "this path is not inevitable," pointing to Meta delaying Muse for months, the SI Accords, and Mark Zuckerberg's call for labs to spend most compute serving customers rather than on self-improvement.

Mental Math

  1. 19 × 5 = ?
  2. 65% of 560 = ?
  3. Fermi: a sheet of printer paper is about 0.1 mm thick. How tall is a stack of 10 reams of 500 sheets?
Show answer
  1. 95 19 × 5 = 10 × 5 + 9 × 5 = 95
  2. 364 10% is 56, so 65% = 6 × 56 + half of 56 = 364
  3. about 0.5 m 10 × 500 = 5,000 sheets × 0.1 mm = 500 mm = 0.5 m

Four Cards

If a parcel is marked Express, it has a red sticker.

Red stickerStandardBlue stickerExpress

Each card has one thing on each side. Which cards must you turn over to check the rule?

Show answer

Turn Express and Blue sticker. Express could hide something that breaks the rule; Blue sticker breaks it if the other side turns out to be Express. Red sticker can't break it (the rule never says only those cards have it), and Standard isn't covered by the rule at all. Most people pick the card that matches the rule's second half instead of the one that could falsify it.
