Petition to reinstate Jensen Huang tops 65,000 signatures as investors press Nvidia's board

An open letter calling for Huang's return as Nvidia CEO, arguing he was removed "without meaningful due process," has passed 65,000 signatures, including more than 35,000 shareholders and 4,700 current and former employees, @KobeissiLetter reports. Elon Musk says he signed it and questioned the board's credibility. @GavinSBaker says Atreides sent its own letter to Nvidia's board, a first for the firm, setting out what he and @altcap call minimum acceptable standards, while also backing reinstatement pending the independent investigation. @jukan05 hears from industry insiders that rivals are already calling Nvidia's veteran engineers, many of whom stayed mainly for Huang.

Overnight Starlink launch adds up to 26 Tb/s; Musk says Starship flights will carry 50+

The Starlink payload launched overnight adds up to 26 terabits per second of capacity to the constellation, and Elon Musk says future Starship flights will carry more than 50 Tb/s of Starlink bandwidth to orbit each. Separately, @mvcinvesting notes Rocket Lab completed its 100th Electron mission, the fastest commercially developed rocket to reach that mark.

Investors buy the dip: global equity funds take in $98 billion in a week

Global equity funds drew $98 billion in the week to Nov. 11, their second-largest weekly inflow on record and fifth straight weekly intake (about $350 billion in total), @KobeissiLetter reports. The four-week average, about $75 billion, is the highest on record, versus a peak near $45 billion during the 2021 meme-stock mania; tech funds added $2.6 billion.

Four Cards

If a day's forecast says rain, the card shows an umbrella on the back.

SnowRainUmbrellaScarf

Each card has one thing on each side. Which cards must you turn over to check the rule?

Show answer

Turn Rain and Scarf. Rain could hide something that breaks the rule; Scarf breaks it if the other side turns out to be Rain. Umbrella can't break it (the rule never says only those cards have it), and Snow isn't covered by the rule at all. Most people pick the card that matches the rule's second half instead of the one that could falsify it.
