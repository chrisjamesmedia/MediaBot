Front Page

SpaceX catches a returning Starship in the tower arms for the first time

On Flight 15, Ship 42 came back from orbit to Starbase, Texas and was caught by the launch tower's "chopsticks," the first-ever Starship tower catch. Elon Musk shared a video he said "gives a clear sense of the catch maneuver" and later phone videos he took from the tower. @wintonARK calls it the largest object ever safely returned from orbit and argues it drops the cost per kilogram to orbit to roughly $100, from $1,000. @sriramk: "a testament to what mankind can accomplish."

Judge lets DRAM antitrust suit against Samsung, SK hynix and Micron go ahead

A federal court in California found the plaintiffs plausibly allege that Samsung, SK hynix and Micron agreed to restrict DRAM supply, and denied the companies' motion to dismiss, @jukan05 reports. He calls it a stretch: asking customers to verify end demand, he argues, was meant to stop resellers double-booking orders, not collusion. Separately, Korean media report SK hynix plans to add at least 20,000 wafers a month of HBM4 capacity, citing strong demand from AMD and Broadcom.

Gene Munster: Nvidia may not give guidance when it reports Tuesday night

@munster_gene gives two reasons Nvidia may skip guidance: management changes make it hard for the new CFO to own the forecast, and tighter US Commerce restrictions could put Asia sales at risk, by his estimate about 10% of revenue (China and Hong Kong 8%, other 2%), with some estimates as high as 20%. He says that could pressure the shares next week.

From Your X

THThe Humanoid Hub@TheHumanoidHub · Nov 15

OpenAI has disbanded its stand-alone robotics team and reassigned the researchers to its general AI teams, even as it keeps building its own humanoid robot, with a first public demo targeted for 2027. The move, first reported in a Fortune exclusive, is framed by OpenAI as acceleration, not retreat. A spokesperson said "robotics remains a core part of OpenAI's long-term vision," and that the company "remains committed to bringing general-purpose robots into the world." The real shift is the bet underneath it: that general-purpose models can run robots directly, with no specialized "robot brain" needed. In an Nvidia study, an agent powered by OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra hit 74.8% on a simulated manipulation benchmark, vs 51.0% for the best specialized robotics models. If that holds, it challenges the premise many robotics startups raised billions on: that robots need brains of their own.

Four Cards

If a card has a vowel on one side, it has an even number on the other.

4E3K

Each card has one thing on each side. Which cards must you turn over to check the rule?

Show answer

Turn E and 3. E could hide something that breaks the rule; 3 breaks it if the other side turns out to be E. 4 can't break it (the rule never says only those cards have it), and K isn't covered by the rule at all. Most people pick the card that matches the rule's second half instead of the one that could falsify it.
