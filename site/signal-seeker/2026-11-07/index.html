FLFred Lambert@FredLambert · Nov 6

Tesla just confirmed it will end Cybertruck production in 2027. I think it's one of the best case studies we have on managing customer expectations. Tesla received more than 1 million reservations for the Cybertruck. Our best estimate puts the real number closer to 2 million. That's demand most automakers can only dream of. Tesla delivered about 70,000 trucks in three years. You can point to a lot of factors: the design, the production ramp, quality issues, the brand's politics, competition. They all played a role. But they're small compared to the main one. Tesla promised a $40,000 truck with up to 500 miles of range. It delivered an $80,000 truck with about 300 miles of range. Those 1 million+ people didn't reserve the truck Tesla ended up building. They reserved a promise. When the product showed up at twice the price with 40% less range, most of them simply walked away.

Mental Math

  1. 21 × 8 = ?
  2. 45% of 220 = ?
  3. Fermi: a sheet of printer paper is about 0.1 mm thick. How tall is a stack of 20 reams of 500 sheets?
Show answer
  1. 168 21 × 8 = 20 × 8 + 1 × 8 = 168
  2. 99 10% is 22, so 45% = 4 × 22 + half of 22 = 99
  3. about 1 m 20 × 500 = 10,000 sheets × 0.1 mm = 1,000 mm = 1 m

Four Cards

If a person is drinking beer, they must be over 21.

Drinking waterAge 25Age 17Drinking beer

Each card has one thing on each side. Which cards must you turn over to check the rule?

Show answer

Turn Drinking beer and Age 17. Drinking beer could hide something that breaks the rule; Age 17 breaks it if the other side turns out to be Drinking beer. Age 25 can't break it (the rule never says only those cards have it), and Drinking water isn't covered by the rule at all. Most people pick the card that matches the rule's second half instead of the one that could falsify it. Most people get this version right and the letters-and-numbers version wrong, though the logic is identical.
