Front Page

Report: Nvidia's board is preparing to oust Jensen Huang

A Wall Street Journal report that Nvidia's board is preparing to remove Huang sent the stock down about 8% after hours, says @GavinSBaker, who argues the board should finish its independent investigation first, or put any earlier move to a shareholder vote. By early Thursday @KobeissiLetter reported the stock had climbed about 6% off its low, still roughly 5% under Wednesday's close, as traders pared bets on a firing. @naval: "one does not just simply replace the founder."

X @GavinSBaker · X @KobeissiLetter · X @naval

Nebius Q3: $981M revenue, and prepayments now cover more than the capex

Nebius reported Q3 revenue of about $981 million against roughly $908 million expected, per @KevInvestingYT and @EndicottInvests. @demian_ai's recap of the release: customers committed more than $12 billion since the start of the quarter, prepayments covered over 100% of the capex behind Q3 deals (50-60% three months earlier), GPU-hour prices rose 22% on average from Q2, and Databricks, Datadog and Snowflake signed for the first time. @EndicottInvests reports 2027 capex guidance of $40-45 billion, at least $18.5 billion of it expected from prepayments.

X @demian_ai · X @EndicottInvests · X @KevInvestingYT

Jukan: Nvidia's Rubin slips again, volume shipments now 2027

Following a Taiwan rumor that Nvidia had cut orders, @jukan05 says he has confirmed that Rubin has been delayed again, this time by motherboard warpage (the HBM issue was resolved in October), pushing volume shipments to 2027; Nvidia has raised Blackwell orders in the meantime. A source told him a widely shared claim of 30% CCL order cuts was false.

X @jukan05

Tesla Robotaxi now runs 24/7 in every city it serves

Tesla's @robotaxi account says the service now runs around the clock in all of its cities, which @elonmusk put as "168 hours a week." Until now only Austin had defined operating hours, notes @TSLAFanMtl, who reads the move as a sign of confidence in FSD and less human supervision; the next thing to watch is fleet growth in each city.

X @elonmusk · X @TSLAFanMtl

Who decides Jensen Huang's fate at $NVDA? X is full of strong opinions. Let's look at the facts. Then I'll share what I believe is currently happening behind the scenes. There are 11 directors on the board. Jensen is the only one who isn't independent. Removing him takes a simple majority for the decision: 6 votes. Nvidia has no chair. Lead independent director Stephen Neal (77, a white-collar defense lawyer) runs the process. The 10 independent directors (number in bracket represents an estimate how much money they made from NVDIA stock). The founding loyalists: 1) Mark Stevens (since 1993, ~$7.5B): Sequoia partner when it backed Nvidia in 1993. Sold ~$1.4B of stock this year. 2) Tench Coxe (1993, ~$5.9B): Sutter Hill co-led Nvidia's first $2M round. 3) Harvey Jones (1993, ~$1.6B): advised Jensen in the earliest days. 4) Brooke Seawell (1997, ~$0.6B): has chaired the audit committee for decades. Jensen made them ~$15.6B. They've known Jensen for 30+ years. The swing votes: 5) Stephen Neal (2019, ~$38M): his old firm Cooley has been Nvidia's outside counsel since 1993, but he's a career white-collar investigator. 6) Dawn Hudson (2013, ~$162M): chairs the committee that sets Jensen's pay. 7) Aarti Shah (2020, ~$12M): her son works at Nvidia. The independents: 8) John Dabiri (2020, ~$6M): Caltech academic. 9) Melissa Lora (2023, ~$3M): has never sold a share. 10) Suzanne Nora Johnson (July 2026, ~$0): joined from Goldman Sachs. Ousting Jensen needs all 3 independents AND all 3 swing votes, or at least 1 founding loyalist turning on him. The board is reportedly "preparing to oust" him. If that's true, someone from the old guard may already have flipped. My personal view: The board knows full well it is clearly in Nvidia's economic interest to keep Jensen for as long as they can. Jensen is Nvidia. He is their vision, their heart, their foundation. And he personally owns many of their most important relationships. I think they will do everything in their power to keep him in the CEO role, within the laws and rules they are bound by. Their key task will be to find plausible deniability for keeping him, without violating any rules or laws themselves. Their incentive to keep him is 100% clear to me. The question is whether they can justify it without putting themselves at risk of neglecting their legal or board duties.

Joe Hudson's 7 operating rules

Codie Sanchez @Codie_Sanchez

Mental Math

  1. 20 × 6 = ?
  2. 65% of 240 = ?
  3. Fermi: a sheet of printer paper is about 0.1 mm thick. How tall is a stack of 20 reams of 500 sheets?
Show answer
  1. 120 20 × 6 = 20 × 6 + 0 × 6 = 120
  2. 156 10% is 24, so 65% = 6 × 24 + half of 24 = 156
  3. about 1 m 20 × 500 = 10,000 sheets × 0.1 mm = 1,000 mm = 1 m

Four Cards

If a person is drinking beer, they must be over 21.

Drinking sodaAge 16Age 40Drinking beer

Each card has one thing on each side. Which cards must you turn over to check the rule?

Show answer

Turn Drinking beer and Age 16. Drinking beer could hide something that breaks the rule; Age 16 breaks it if the other side turns out to be Drinking beer. Age 40 can't break it (the rule never says only those cards have it), and Drinking soda isn't covered by the rule at all. Most people pick the card that matches the rule's second half instead of the one that could falsify it. Most people get this version right and the letters-and-numbers version wrong, though the logic is identical.
