Tesla ships FSD V15, and the feed spends the night watching its new Reality View

Tesla's V15 release notes describe a rebuilt end-to-end network: a vision encoder shared by FSD, Robotaxi and Optimus, trained on internet video, that they say cuts the critical intervention rate 25x, and a single "Reality View" visualization that uses less total compute. @elonmusk called it "a banger release" and clarified overnight that "we mean 25 times," not 25%. Tesla AI's @aelluswamy said the view is "produced end-to-end, pixels-in-pixels-out," and owners spent the night posting clips of it rendering Toronto streetcars and a historic church.

Musk: next year's AI cash crunch will be "nuts"

"Getting this amount of compute turned on, cooled and working reliably is incredibly hard," @elonmusk wrote Saturday; even a few months' lag between paying for compute and earning on it "will mean hundreds of billions of incremental dollars just for working capital. Where will that come from?" Skeptics like @rdd147 read it as a call against the neoclouds; @SMASIMHO answered with Nebius's Roman Chernin: "If demand is there, the working capital problem can be solved."

Why Nebius's power curve matters more than its size

Melvin @MelvinInvests

A walk through one forecast chart: Nebius's active power growing from 170 MW in 2025 to about 5 GW by 2033, with revenue per megawatt projected to rise from $3.2 million to $14.9 million by 2029, passing CoreWeave's projected peak. The useful idea is the framing: power sets how many GPUs you can run, but revenue per megawatt says whether customers fill them. It is a bull case built on a forecast, worth rereading against this week's neocloud selloff.

  1. 25 × 4 = ?
  2. 45% of 620 = ?
  3. Fermi: a resting heart beats about 65 times a minute. Roughly how many beats in a year?
Show answer
  1. 100 25 × 4 = 20 × 4 + 5 × 4 = 100
  2. 279 10% is 62, so 45% = 4 × 62 + half of 62 = 279
  3. about 34 million 65 × 60 × 24 × 365 = 34,164,000

Four Cards

If a person is drinking beer, they must be over 21.

Age 25Age 17Drinking sodaDrinking beer

Each card has one thing on each side. Which cards must you turn over to check the rule?

Show answer

Turn Drinking beer and Age 17. Drinking beer could hide something that breaks the rule; Age 17 breaks it if the other side turns out to be Drinking beer. Age 25 can't break it (the rule never says only those cards have it), and Drinking soda isn't covered by the rule at all. Most people pick the card that matches the rule's second half instead of the one that could falsify it. Most people get this version right and the letters-and-numbers version wrong, though the logic is identical.
