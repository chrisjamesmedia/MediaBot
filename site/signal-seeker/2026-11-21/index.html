An Anthropic mathematician says he and Claude proved the Riemann Hypothesis

Levent Alpöge (@__alpoge__) posted a claimed proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, written with Claude and published as a formal proof in Lean, @HarryStoltz1 flagged early Friday; @sciam called it a claim on "the most notorious open problem in all of math." @MattPirkowski noted the proof runs to more than 250,000 pages' worth of machine-checkable code, so no human, its author included, has read it end to end. @sundarpichai's "Euler, Gauss and Riemann in awe" was the most-liked reaction; by Saturday morning @elonmusk was hedging: "Even if Riemann turns out not to be fully solved yet, it will be soon enough."

A week for the record books, read as a leverage unwind rather than a crisis

Mental Math

  1. 20 × 7 = ?
  2. 85% of 420 = ?
  3. Fermi: you read 200 words a minute for 15 minutes a day. How many days for a 120,000-word book?
Show answer
  1. 140 20 × 7 = 20 × 7 + 0 × 7 = 140
  2. 357 10% is 42, so 85% = 8 × 42 + half of 42 = 357
  3. 40 days 200 × 15 = 3,000 words a day; 120,000 ÷ 3,000 = 40.0, so 40 days

Four Cards

If a card is red on one side, it has a circle on the other.

RedCircleTriangleGreen

Each card has one thing on each side. Which cards must you turn over to check the rule?

Show answer

Turn Red and Triangle. Red could hide something that breaks the rule; Triangle breaks it if the other side turns out to be Red. Circle can't break it (the rule never says only those cards have it), and Green isn't covered by the rule at all. Most people pick the card that matches the rule's second half instead of the one that could falsify it.
