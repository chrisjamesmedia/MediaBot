BBalaji@balajis · Nov 23

Founders produce higher returns than non-founders. This is an empirical fact. So: business school curricula should be changed, and good governance should be redefined as founder control and dual-class stock. Shareholders are still protected by easy liquidation and their ability to fund new entrants. After all, you don’t invest in a company based on who’s on the board. That’s largely ceremonial. The key difference from the 20th century is that it was prohibitively difficult in 1950 to found a manufacturing company, because assembly lines cost so much, the communists controlled much of the world, and even America had 90% marginal tax rates. It was the century of centralization, the individual powerless before the institution. Today it is no longer prohibitively difficult to found a tech company, because software costs so little, much of the world has turned to free markets, and capital flows at the speed of the Internet. This is the century of decentralization, at least in the West, with the institution falling to the individual. For example: Anthropic was started just a few years ago and is beating even Google at its own game. If we get many open weights models, their moat will also in turn erode. So: all 20th century corporate governance was based on the idea that “the company is hard to replace, but its CEO isn’t.” The Harvard MBA is premised on the professional manager. It was expensive to start a new company with all the assembly lines, so make sure it doesn’t fail by making every employee replaceable. Managers were abundant so capital became the scarce asset. By contrast, the entire 21st century model is based on the idea that “A tech founder is hard to replace, but the company isn’t.” The YCombinator model is premised on the startup founder. It is inexpensive to start a new company with a few lines of code, so make sure it doesn’t fail by having an irreplaceable team. Capital is abundant so founders become the scarce asset.

Mental Math

  1. 16 × 8 = ?
  2. 85% of 420 = ?
  3. Fermi: you read 200 words a minute for 20 minutes a day. How many days for a 60,000-word book?
Show answer
  1. 128 16 × 8 = 10 × 8 + 6 × 8 = 128
  2. 357 10% is 42, so 85% = 8 × 42 + half of 42 = 357
  3. 15 days 200 × 20 = 4,000 words a day; 60,000 ÷ 4,000 = 15.0, so 15 days

Four Cards

If a day's forecast says rain, the card shows an umbrella on the back.

UmbrellaSunglassesSunnyRain

Each card has one thing on each side. Which cards must you turn over to check the rule?

Show answer

Turn Rain and Sunglasses. Rain could hide something that breaks the rule; Sunglasses breaks it if the other side turns out to be Rain. Umbrella can't break it (the rule never says only those cards have it), and Sunny isn't covered by the rule at all. Most people pick the card that matches the rule's second half instead of the one that could falsify it.
