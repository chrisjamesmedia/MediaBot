SpaceX will give every American child two shares

SpaceX said it is committing one share to millions of American children, to give the next generation a stake in building the future. Hours later Elon Musk added that every child in America will receive two shares, one from the company and one from him personally; per @altcap, the shares go to children under 18 who have claimed their 530A account. @GavinSBaker called it great for the country and the best way to teach a generation about capitalism, and asked more founders to do the same.

Karpathy has Claude turn US public data into a Civilization map

@karpathy asked Claude to populate a Civilization-style map with real US data. Seven hours and 45,000 lines of code later it works: 31 sources (Census, BEA, BLS, NOAA, USGS, FAA, CDC and more) reconciled onto one 25 km hex grid, with GDP as gold yield, cities' districts drawn from their real industries, and 19 lenses from income to faith to disasters.

X @karpathy

Polymarket opens a market in the price of GPU time

@shayne_coplan said Polymarket now prices on-demand compute for Nvidia H100s, B200s and B300s out to 2028, a kind of yield curve for the cost of compute that lenders, labs and providers could hedge against. @travisk called GPU futures critical to compute financing. Separately, @SemiAnalysis_ said Google's TPU and Amazon's Trainium are now on its InferenceX benchmark, at 1.2x to 2.5x the performance per dollar of Nvidia GPUs.

Four Cards

If a card is red on one side, it has a circle on the other.

SquareBlueRedCircle

Each card has one thing on each side. Which cards must you turn over to check the rule?

Show answer

Turn Red and Square. Red could hide something that breaks the rule; Square breaks it if the other side turns out to be Red. Circle can't break it (the rule never says only those cards have it), and Blue isn't covered by the rule at all. Most people pick the card that matches the rule's second half instead of the one that could falsify it.
