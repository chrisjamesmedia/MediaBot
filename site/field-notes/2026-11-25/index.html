Math Pulse @themathpulse

A town where everyone uses “word A” for something, say what to call a street. Now add a few people who use “word B” and never change, whatever anyone says.

Does the town change its word?

With 5% stubborn people, word B ends at only 6%. With 16%, it reaches 100%. Repeated runs put the tipping point near 10%, and the closer the stubborn share is to it, the longer the crowd takes to decide.

This is the naming game, studied by physicists and linguists as a model of how conventions form.

The surprise is how small the minority can be: nobody has to be persuaded by argument. They only have to be certain, and let everyone else's habits do the rest.
